I have five years of technical writing behind me. Until now, most of that work lived inside company tools where nobody outside could see it.

So I am changing that. From today, I build in public.

<!-- truncate -->

## What I am building

There are two projects, and both document the same fictional product. It is a loyalty rewards platform called LoyaltyLoop.

**Project 1** is a complete API documentation set. Quickstart, authentication, concepts, reference, errors, webhooks, batch jobs, and how-to guides. Every example will run against a mock server before it goes live.

**Project 2** is an AI documentation pipeline. It uses the API spec from Project 1 to generate new pages with prompt templates. Then it tests those prompts, checks the output automatically, and puts a human review at the end.

You can follow both on the [Portfolio](/portfolio) page.

## Why one product

Real documentation work is never a pile of unrelated samples. It is one product, many readers, and pages that have to agree with each other.

Using one product for both projects lets me show that. It also lets the AI project learn from pages I wrote by hand.

## What else is coming

The [Learning Hub](/learn) gets a new lesson every week. Each lesson has a matching video on YouTube, with short tips in between.

The lessons come from what I am learning right now: Git, APIs, Python, SQL, and docs-as-code. I explain them the way I wish someone had explained them to me.

## Why share it at all

Because learning alone is slow, and teaching is how I learn best. It has been that way since my years in the classroom.

If you are a writer trying to get closer to the technical side, come along. We will figure it out together.
